IMPLEMENTATION GUIDE · MODEL CARD · DATA LINEAGE

What is happening,
step by step.

From a challenge with no supplied dataset to autonomous local agents operating a counterfactual payment-network twin, an explainable model, graph-aware defense, and a governed learning loop.

Evidence boundary: current results come from a fictional synthetic sandbox. Public datasets are reference-only; real efficacy needs an authorized shadow-mode pilot.

STEP 01

Turn the brief into a closed defense loop

The challenge asks teams to identify emerging AI-enabled payment attacks, generate faithful simulations, and defend with AI/ML while controlling false positives. FraudGuard connects those requirements as Identify → Generate → Defend → Learn.

Creative adversarial research stays offline. The live-style path remains deterministic: feature calculation, locked inference, graph context, policy thresholds, and auditable reason codes.

STEP 02

Use a hybrid evidence stack honestly

The Kaggle challenge supplies no dataset. The runnable system therefore generates fictional transactions from 22 governed scenarios, fixed seeds, explicit synthetic labels, monotonic event time, and hard negatives that resemble fraud.

LayerStatusWhat it contributes
Payment-network twinActiveNovel attacks, labels, counterfactuals, hard negatives
Public benchmarksReference onlySchema, imbalance, temporal, and graph realism anchors
Institution aggregatesPilot requiredCalibration, drift, thresholds, business cost

No raw PAN, credential, or customer PII belongs in the lab. Naming a public reference does not mean it was ingested or used for training.

GitHub dataset: download the complete 210,000-row archive from data/releases/, inspect the 4,200-row JSONL sample in data/sample/, and verify composition and SHA-256 checksums in data/README.md.

STEP 03

Fork normal and attacked worlds

src/twin-engine.js creates customers, merchants, devices, payments, and relationships. Each exercise creates a low-fraud baseline and an attacked branch under the same high-level scenario controls.

The attacker varies bounded pressure and stealth. Profiles include authorization drift, probe-and-pivot behavior, identity maturation, collusive networks, split-and-merge paths, dispute loops, payment coercion, and account swarms. These are safe defensive abstractions, not operational instructions.

STEP 04

Identify AI-native campaigns, not just old fraud labels

The threat atlas contains 24 campaigns over 22 payment-attack families. Each records the AI enabler, affected payment surfaces, a safe four-stage observable kill chain, novelty and difficulty, six signal domains, and expected controls.

The familiar fraud family remains underneath as a tested generator primitive. Novelty comes from planning, coordination, adaptation, channel movement, and evidence manipulation added by the campaign layer.

STEP 05

Let local agents produce evidence, not theatre

src/agent-lab.js runs six explicit roles: a threat scout selects a campaign, a planner proposes three bounded policies, an operator executes each policy in a fresh synthetic twin, a critic scores observed outcomes, an evolver retains the winner, and a blue sentinel strengthens graph defense for the next generation.

The agents optimize a chosen defensive-research objective using actual escape, graph-lift, friction, pressure, and stealth measurements. Their memory contains only the winning bounded policy. The visible trace is an action-and-observation audit log—not hidden reasoning. No hosted model is required, and the action space contains no network, credential, customer-data, or payment-rail access.

STEP 06

Start with an explainable benchmark

Logistic · active

Fast, inspectable, locked JSON artifact, no ML runtime dependency.

Boosted trees · challenger

Benchmark LightGBM/XGBoost for nonlinear local risk.

Temporal graph · next layer

Learn entity and sequence risk once graph evidence is calibrated.

The active NumPy-trained artifact stores feature order, normalization, coefficients, threshold, split manifest, metrics, limitations, and a content-derived model version. Rules remain a safe fallback.

STEP 07

Expose the relationship a payment hides

The fast model scores velocity, amount deviation, device, location, payee, account, identity, and merchant context. The arena separately measures the simulated graph motif—shared devices, layering, fan-out, recurrence, and community behavior—then fuses both signals into ALLOW, STEP_UP, REVIEW, or BLOCK.

Each decision receipt keeps the transaction score, graph score, reason codes, model version, feature version, and synthetic flag. Graph fusion can be switched off to create a clean comparison.

STEP 08

Measure three rounds, not one flattering score

POST /api/v1/arena/run returns a counterfactual normal round, transaction-only attack round, and graph-aware adapted round. The website visualizes the returned entity graph and timeline directly.

Outputs include payment value, detected and escaped attacks, prevented-value lift, exposure reduction, customer-friction rate, estimated fusion latency, attacker/defender advantage, and decision receipts. These comparative synthetic metrics are useful for design decisions—not production performance claims.

STEP 09

Learn without uncontrolled self-modification

The mutation loop finds actual false negatives and proposes traceable stress variants. Every batch is HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED. A replay never retrains, changes, or promotes the active model.

Miss → traceable variants → candidate artifact → locked comparison → human approval → promote or reject → rollback available
STEP 10

Move toward production in controlled stages

  1. Freeze the benchmark and compare model challengers.
  2. Calibrate against approved aggregate evidence.
  3. Run shadow scoring with no payment impact.
  4. Recommend step-up authentication at medium risk.
  5. Enable selective high-confidence blocking with rollback.
  6. Monitor drift, fairness, latency, friction, and prevented loss.

Before any pilot, add schema validation, durable state, authentication, artifact signing, audit storage, privacy review, and model-risk approval.
